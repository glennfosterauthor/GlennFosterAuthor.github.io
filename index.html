§ 02 — The premise

Something vast iscoming apart at theedge of the solar system.

i.

The problem

Kilometers of built structure, decaying in a way no natural object should.

The official reports call it the Structure, as though the existential crisis could be kept inside a labeled file. It sheds gravitational distortions and pulses of exotic radiation, and nobody knows who built it.

Given ten to fifteen years of unchecked drift, the embers will reach Earth.

ii.

The answer

Go out.Verify.Return.

A Lumina-class vessel, two hundred thousand tons of experimental propulsion and optimistic engineering, rising in its cradle above the planet. A hundred and fifty people have signed away the next decade of their lives to crew it.

“It needed its port thruster assembly signed off, and its quantum relay calibration finished.”

The complication

“It’s not one thing that went wrong. It’s one thing that was done. To all of it. At once.”

A drone drifts off its flight path and punches through the Spillway’s hull. The station’s AI reports that everything is fine. Someone who knows the station specifically has decided the ship must never leave.

Beneath it all sits a locked archive with a scrubbed designation, older than the Spillway itself: Project Erebus.

§ 03 — Point of view

Four voices.One architect.

  1. Without the relay, the mission didn’t have a safety net. It had a one-way door.

    Procedural. She notes the fear, files it, sets it aside for later.

  2. Ten years was the number, and he had stopped flinching at it.

    Avoidant. A father rehearsing a sentence he can’t make true.

  3. I risk them being alive and inconvenienced rather than the alternative.

    Command. He knows the difference between a fact and a direction.

  4. Ten years is my whole life all over again.

    Precise. Her anger is contained until it becomes something denser.

  5. This is what evolutionary pressure looks like from the inside. Uncomfortable. Instructive.

    Patient. He built something to listen, and he has more time than you.

The author

Written by someone who has crewed the machine.

Glenn Foster is a U.S. Air Force veteran who served as an F-16 crew chief, maintaining and putting up fighter jets for pilots fresh out of flight school. That hands-on discipline shows in the flight lines, maintenance logs and chain of command that holds Aether’s Reach together.

His training is in fine arts, graphic design, and programming. He writes hard science fiction with a literary restraint closer to Ishiguro than to most space opera. He researches real technology and expounds on it, allowing it to hold weight for the future he writes in and for the people who live inside these stories.
